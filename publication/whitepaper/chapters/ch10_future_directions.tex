\section{Sub-1.5 BPW Training and the 1.0 BPW Barrier}
\label{sec:sub_1_5_bpw}
Recall the quantisation barrier of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant_barrier}: at
1\,BPW, each weight carries one bit---at most two representable values.
The gradient dead zone grows superlinearly below 1.5\,BPW because codebook
resolution cannot capture fractional weight updates.
\begin{mythm}[Sub-1.5 BPW Gradient Collapse]{thm:sub15_gradient}
Let $\codebook$ be a codebook of size $|\codebook| = 2^{b}$ for $b < 1.5$.
For any Lipschitz-continuous loss $\mathcal{L}$, there exists an open
neighbourhood of every weight vector $\mathbf{w}$ such that all stochastic
gradient updates map to $\mathbf{w}$ under OIL quantisation. The Lebesgue
measure of this neighbourhood grows monotonically as $b \to 0$.
\end{mythm}
Sub-1.5\,BPW training is \emph{measure-theoretically} obstructed: the set
of useful gradient directions has vanishing measure. At 1.0 BPW the
codebook collapses to $\{-c, +c\}$, a binary sign-alternating
representation. Gradient flow through the sign function is discontinuous
almost everywhere.
\begin{mythm}[The 1.0 BPW Barrier]{thm:one_bpw_barrier}
Let $\mathcal{C}_{\text{binary}} = \{-c, +c\}$ encode 1.0 BPW, and let
$\mathcal{L}$ be $L$-Lipschitz. For gradient descent $\mathbf{w}' =
\mathbf{w} - \eta \nabla \mathcal{L}$, the probability that
$\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{w}') \neq \mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{w})$ satisfies
$\mathbb{P}(\text{flip}) \leq \frac{\eta L}{2c}$. As $c \to \infty$ or
$\eta \to 0$, the probability of any weight update vanishes.
\end{mythm}
\begin{proofsketch}
An update crosses the sign threshold when $\eta |\nabla\mathcal{L}| >
|\mathbf{w}|$. By $L$-Lipschitz continuity, $|\nabla\mathcal{L}| \leq L$,
so $\text{measure}(|\mathbf{w}| < \eta L) / (2c) = \eta L / (2c)$.
\end{proofsketch}
This barrier is fundamental: a stronger characterisation exposes the
precise capacity constraint on gradient flow.
\begin{mythm}[Gradient Capacity Bottleneck at 1.0 BPW]{thm:capacity_bottleneck}
Let $\mathbf{w} \in \{\pm c\}^d$ be binary weights and
$\mathbf{g} = \nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})$. Under OIL quantisation the
effective update is $\Delta\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{0}$ almost surely when
$\|\mathbf{g}\|_\infty < c/\eta$. When a flip occurs, the update direction
is $\operatorname{sgn}(\mathbf{g})$ but magnitude is fixed at $2c$, yielding
$\mathbb{E}[\langle \Delta\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{g} \rangle] \leq
2c\|\mathbf{g}\|_1$. The fraction of coordinates that can change per step
is at most $\eta\|\mathbf{g}\|_1 / (c d)$.
\end{mythm}
\begin{proofsketch}
$\eta|g_i| < c$ implies no flip, so $\mathbf{1}_{\{\text{flip}\}} \leq
\eta|g_i|/c$. The inner product becomes $2c\sum_i \operatorname{sgn}(g_i)g_i
\mathbf{1}_{\{\text{flip}\}} \leq 2c\sum_i |g_i| \cdot \eta|g_i|/c =
2\eta\|g\|_2^2$, and counting coordinates with $|g_i| \geq c/\eta$ gives
the fraction bound.
\end{proofsketch}
This formalises a no-free-lunch property: binary networks must either
accept frozen weights or use biased surrogate gradients---the Ternary
codebook at 1.58\,BPW avoids this via a zero centre preserving a
non-vanishing gradient channel. The lottery ticket hypothesis
~\cite{frankle2019} is closely related: at very low BPW only weights
escaping the gradient dead zone participate in learning.
\begin{mycor}[Active Weight Fraction]{cor:active_weight}
Let $f_{\text{active}}$ be the fraction of weights whose gradient magnitude
exceeds the codebook step $\Delta$. For width-$n$ network at BPW $b$,
$f_{\text{active}} = O(n^{-\alpha(b)})$ where $\alpha(b)$ increases as
$b$ decreases below 1.5.
\end{mycor}

\section{Codebook Learning Theory}
\label{sec:codebook_learning}
Codebooks in current OIL are designed by quantile binning of the initial
weight distribution. Learning codebooks from data generalises this with
connections to vector quantisation and rate-distortion theory.
\subsection{Excess Risk for Learned Codebooks}
When centroids are estimated from finite samples, the generalisation gap
must be controlled. The hypothesis class is the set of $K$-point quantisers.
\begin{mythm}[Codebook Learnability Bound]{thm:codebook_learnability}
Let $\mathcal{C}_N$ be a $K$-entry codebook learned from $N$ i.i.d.\ weight
samples with population distortion $D^* = \min_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbb{E}[ \|
\mathbf{w} - \mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{w}) \|^2 ]$. Then with probability at least
$1 - \delta$,
$\mathbb{E}[\| \mathbf{w} - \mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{C}_N}(\mathbf{w}) \|^2] -
D^* \leq O\big( \sqrt{ (K d \log N + \log(1/\delta))/N } \big),$
where $d$ is the weight dimension and $K = 2^b$.
\end{mythm}
Convergence to the population-optimal quantiser follows from uniform
convergence of the distortion functional over the class of $K$-centre sets.
The bound reveals a fundamental tension: larger $K$ (higher BPW) increases
capacity and thus sample requirement. The reconstruction error can be
sharpened when the weight manifold has low intrinsic dimension, which for
pre-trained networks is empirically much smaller than $d$.
\begin{mythm}[Codebook Reconstruction Error]{thm:codebook_reconstruction}
Let $\mathcal{C}_N$ be a $K$-entry codebook with centres $\{c_1, \dots,
c_K\}$ learned from $N$ samples drawn from a distribution supported on a
$d_{\text{eff}}$-dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^d$ with diameter
$R$. With probability at least $1 - \delta$,
$\mathbb{E}[\| \mathbf{w} - \mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{w}) \|^2] \leq
O\big( R^2 K^{-2/d_{\text{eff}}} + \sqrt{ (K d_{\text{eff}} \log N +
\log(1/\delta))/N } \big).$
\end{mythm}
When $d_{\text{eff}} \ll d$, small $K$ suffices---OIL4 works well because
trained weight manifolds have logarithmic effective dimension.

\section{Adaptive Codebook Scheduling}
\label{sec:adaptive_codebook}
Current OIL uses fixed codebooks determined before training---a significant
limitation. Codebook centres could be optimised end-to-end via gradient
descent, but entries shared across all weights in a layer create coupling
that standard SGD cannot efficiently resolve. A natural approach maintains
a continuous relaxation during training and snaps to discrete values only
in the forward pass, enabling codebook gradients via STE applied to centroid
assignment. For mixed-precision formats like \texttt{OIL\_MIX\_3}, the
codebook can be decomposed hierarchically: a coarse codebook for significant
bits and a fine residual for least significant bits.
\begin{mythm}[Codebook Refinement Monotonicity]{thm:codebook_refine}
Let $\codebook_k$ denote a codebook at refinement stage $k$ with
converged loss $\mathcal{L}_k$. If refinement splits only the centroid
with highest within-cluster variance, then $\mathcal{L}_{k+1} \leq
\mathcal{L}_k$ with equality only when the split centroid has zero
variance.
\end{mythm}
Refinement admits a Bayesian interpretation: splitting a centroid
corresponds to a prior-to-posterior update where posterior concentration
scales with variance explained. In distributed settings, transmitting
codebook updates can dominate communication; adaptive scheduling that
amortises synchronisation over multiple gradient steps---bounding staleness
error as $O(\tau\Delta^2)$ for staleness $\tau$---is an open problem.

\section{Architecture Co-Design}
\label{sec:architecture_codesign}
Attention heads are sensitive to small weight perturbations. OIL allows
per-component precision: OIL4 for feed-forward matrices, OIL8 for attention
projections, OIL32 for layer norm, exploiting differing sensitivity profiles.
State-space architectures such as Mamba~\cite{gu2024} use structured state
matrices with recurrence rather than attention, exhibiting smoother loss
landscapes under weight perturbation and greater tolerance of aggressive
quantisation. MoE models allow different experts to use different formats.
\begin{mythm}[MoE Format Heterogeneity Bound]{thm:moe_het}
Let $E$ experts use formats $b_1,\dots,b_E$. The aggregate loss satisfies
$\mathcal{L} \leq \sum_{i=1}^{E} p_i \cdot \mathcal{L}(b_i)$ where $p_i$
is the routing probability and $\mathcal{L}(b_i)$ the achievable loss at
precision $b_i$.
\end{mythm}

\section{Rate-Distortion Theory for Quantised Training}
\label{sec:rate_distortion}
For a weight source $\mathbf{w} \sim p(\mathbf{w})$ with variance $\sigma^2$,
the rate-distortion function for MSE under a $K$-level quantiser is
$R(D) = \frac{1}{2} \log_2(\sigma^2 / D)$ for $D \leq \sigma^2$.
\begin{mythm}[OIL Rate-Distortion Bound]{thm:rate_distortion}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be any codebook with $|\mathcal{C}| = 2^b$ and maximal
centroid spacing $\Delta_{\max}$. For any weight distribution with finite
second moment $\mathbb{E}[w^2] = \sigma^2$, the quantisation distortion
satisfies $\mathbb{E}[(w - \mathcal{Q}(w))^2] \geq \sigma^2 \cdot 2^{-2b}$.
Equality is achieved iff $\mathcal{C}$ is a Lloyd--Max quantiser for
$p(w)$.
\end{mythm}
This bound connects to the conservation law of Chapter~\ref{ch:conservation}:
$\mathcal{I}_{\text{tot}} = H(\mathbf{w}) - R(D) + \text{const}$, showing
information lost to quantisation is the rate penalty $R(D)$.
\subsection{Rate--Distortion--Perception Tradeoff}
When a perceptual constraint---that the quantised weight distribution should
resemble the original---is imposed, the achievable distortion increases.
\begin{mythm}[Rate--Distortion--Perception for OIL]{thm:rd_perception}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $K$-entry codebook with $K = 2^b$ and let
$\mathcal{P}$ measure the Wasserstein-$2$ distance between $p(w)$ and
$p(\mathcal{Q}(w))$. For any $\mathcal{P} \leq \delta$, the achievable
distortion satisfies $D(b, \delta) \geq \sigma^2 2^{-2b} + \delta^2$.
Conversely, minimising distortion alone yields $\mathcal{P} \geq
\sigma^2 2^{-2b} / K^2$.
\end{mythm}
This three-way tension is central to learned codebooks: refining centroids
to minimise MSE pushes the codebook distribution away from the prior, while
perceptual regularisation pulls it back. Optimal codebooks for downstream
generalisation balance rate, distortion, and perception---an open problem.

\section{Formal Verification of Quantised Training}
\label{sec:formal_verification}
Hardware verification techniques---model checking, SAT solving, zonotope
abstract interpretation---could verify quantised training dynamics by
encoding the OIL map as piecewise-linear and bounding the reachable set of
weight trajectories. Existing theory~\cite{alistarh2018} covers gradient
quantisation, not direct weight quantisation.
\begin{mylemma}[Codebook Constraint Non-Convexity]{lem:codebook_nonconvex}
The feasible set $\mathcal{W}_{\text{OIL}} = \{\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{w}) :
\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n\}$ is non-convex for any codebook with
$|\codebook| \geq 3$ and non-uniform spacing.
\end{mylemma}
Standard convex guarantees do not apply. The ultimate goal is a formal
certificate: ``this model, trained for $T$ steps with format $F$, achieves
loss $\leq \mathcal{L}^*$ with probability $1-\delta$.'' The PAC-Bayes
framework of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_gap} provides a starting point.

Fine codebooks (high BPW) yield low error but high per-step compute; coarse
codebooks (low BPW) are faster per step but require more steps.
\begin{mythm}[Convergence--Complexity Tradeoff]{thm:convergence_complexity}
Under OIL training with codebook step size $\Delta$ and cardinality
$|\codebook| = K$, the iteration complexity to reach $\varepsilon$-stationarity
$\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \mathcal{L}\|] \leq \varepsilon$ satisfies
$\mathcal{T}(\varepsilon, K, \Delta) = O\big(
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{\Delta^2}{\varepsilon^2} +
\frac{K}{\varepsilon} \big).$
The optimal BPW minimising total SOPS satisfies $b^* = \arg\min_b
K(b) \cdot \mathcal{T}(\varepsilon, K(b), \Delta(b))$.
\end{mythm}
Very low BPW may require prohibitive iteration counts despite per-step
efficiency, while very high BPW wastes bandwidth. The sweet spot
OIL4--OIL8 emerges naturally from this tradeoff.

\section{Scaling Laws for Quantised Models}
\label{sec:scaling_laws}
At lower BPW, each parameter carries less information, requiring more
parameters or tokens for the same loss. A preliminary model gives
$\mathcal{L}(N, D, b) \approx A(b)/N^{\alpha(b)} + B(b)/D^{\beta(b)} + E_0$
where $\alpha(b)$ decreases as $b$ decreases. OIL4 may require
$2\times$--$4\times$ more parameters at the same token budget, but total
SOPS may still be lower.
\begin{mycor}[SOPS-Optimal Scaling]{cor:sops_optimal}
For fixed SOPS budget $C$, format $b$ achieves minimum loss with
$N^*(b) > N^*_{\text{FP32}}$ and $D^*(b) < D^*_{\text{FP32}}$ when
$\alpha(b) < \alpha_{\text{FP32}}$.
\end{mycor}

\section{Federated Learning with OIL}
\label{sec:federated_learning}
At OIL4 (0.5 bytes/param), a 1B model fits in 500\,MB---within smartphone
RAM. At OIL2, even 4B parameters fit on-device. OIL quantisation introduces
inherent DP: $\mathcal{Q}$ destroys fine-grained gradient information,
acting as built-in noise with bounded $\ell_\infty$ norm equal to half the
codebook step size.
\begin{mylemma}[OIL Quantisation as DP Mechanism]{lem:oil_dp}
Let $\Delta$ be the maximum codebook step size. OIL provides effective noise
$\sigma_{\text{eff}} \geq \Delta/2$ per update, yielding $(\epsilon,
\delta)$-DP with $\epsilon \leq \frac{\Delta \sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}}
{q \cdot \sigma_g}$ where $q$ is the sampling rate and $\sigma_g$ the
gradient norm bound.
\end{mylemma}
OIL4 reduces gradient communication by $8\times$ versus FP32, making
cross-device federated learning over cellular networks feasible.

\section{Hardware Acceleration and FPGA Co-Design}
\label{sec:hardware_accel}
FPGAs suit OIL inference because codebook lookup maps directly to block RAM
content-addressable memory. An OIL8 implementation stores each 256-entry
codebook in a single BRAM, performing index-to-centroid lookup in one cycle.
\begin{mythm}[FPGA BRAM Throughput Bound]{thm:fpga_throughput}
For an FPGA with $B$ BRAM banks each of width $W$ bits and an OIL format
with $K = 2^b$ entries of $p$ bits each, the maximum throughput is
$\mathcal{T}_{\max} = B \cdot \lfloor W / p \rfloor$ MACs per cycle when
each BRAM bank serves one independent codebook. For OIL4 with $p = 16$,
$W = 36$, and $B = 256$, this yields $256 \cdot 2 = 512$ MACs/cycle.
\end{mythm}
Throughput alone omits energy. The energy per MAC is dominated by
codebook access.
\begin{mythm}[FPGA Energy Scaling]{thm:fpga_energy}
For an OIL accelerator on FPGA, the energy per MAC satisfies
$E_{\text{MAC}} = \alpha b + \beta p + \gamma$, with $E_{\text{BRAM}} \propto
b$, $E_{\text{DSP}} \propto p$, and $E_{\text{route}}$ fixed. For typical
7-series FPGA parameters ($\alpha \approx 0.3$, $\beta \approx 0.5$,
$\gamma \approx 0.2$ pJ), the minimum occurs at OIL4--OIL8.
\end{mythm}
A mid-range FPGA sustains 256 MACs/cycle at 300\,MHz for 76.8\,GOPS---enough
for OIL2 inference on a 7B model at 10 tokens/s. A purpose-built OIL ASIC
would extend this with on-chip SRAM codebooks, hardwired nibble unpackers
for OIL4, and parallel popcount arrays for Ternary GEMM. A 7B OIL4 model
needs only 3.5\,GB, fitting a single HBM stack.

\section{Spiking Neural Network Integration}
\label{sec:snn_integration}
In an SNN, each activation is a spike train $s_i(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ over $T$
timesteps. The weight matrix is OIL-quantised, so the postsynaptic potential
$v_j(t) = \sum_i w_{ji} s_i(t)$ replaces MACs with lookup and accumulate.
\begin{mythm}[SNN Temporal Encoding Distortion]{thm:snn_encoding}
Let $\mathbf{W}$ be OIL-quantised at $b$ BPW and let input spike trains
have firing rate $r \in (0, 1)$. The expected distortion in the
postsynaptic potential over $T$ timesteps satisfies $\mathbb{E}[(\hat{v}_j -
v_j)^2] \leq T r \cdot \sigma^2 2^{-2b}$ where $\sigma^2$ is the weight
variance.
\end{mythm}
Temporal summation mitigates quantisation error: distortion grows linearly
in $T$ while signal grows quadratically ($v_j \propto T r$).
\begin{mythm}[SNN Temporal SNR Scaling]{thm:snn_snr}
For an OIL-quantised SNN at $b$ BPW with input rate $r$ over $T$ timesteps,
the signal-to-noise ratio satisfies $\text{SNR}(T, b) \geq
T r \cdot 2^{2b} / \sigma^2$. Doubling $T$ offsets a $1$-bit reduction in
BPW, revealing a direct tradeoff between temporal integration and weight
precision.
\end{mythm}
Event-driven OIL inference on neuromorphic chips (Loihi, Speck) enables
sub-milliwatt edge deployment. OIL codebooks map to synaptic crossbar
arrays: each entry corresponds to a conductance column, and the OIL index
selects it, achieving $O(1)$ energy per MAC versus $O(b)$ for digital ASICs.
Training SNNs with OIL weights requires addressing non-differentiability of
both spike generation and weight quantisation; a hybrid approach using
surrogate gradients for the spike function while leveraging the conservation
law for weight updates is a promising direction.

\section{Comparison with Competing Approaches}
\label{sec:competing_approaches}
BitNet~\cite{wang2024} trains ternary $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ networks via STE.
OIL differs by providing a format family (1.58--32\,BPW), preserving gradient
flow through codebook centroids rather than STE, and including a formal
information-theoretic framework. QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023} fine-tunes frozen
quantised models with low-rank adapters, while OIL integrates quantisation
\emph{into} training. DoRA~\cite{liu2024} decomposes weights into magnitude
and direction, synergistic with OIL.
\begin{mythm}[OIL vs.\ Post-Training Quantisation]{thm:oil_vs_ptq}
Let $\mathcal{L}_{\text{OIL}}(b)$ be the loss from training in format $b$
and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PTQ}}(b)$ from post-training quantisation. Under CID,
there exists $b^*$ such that $\mathcal{L}_{\text{OIL}}(b) <
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PTQ}}(b)$ for all $b < b^*$.
\end{mythm}

\subsection{Prioritised Roadmap}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Short term (6 months):} Validate scaling laws empirically.
          Implement adaptive codebook scheduling. Benchmark MoE heterogeneity.
          Begin FPGA prototyping for OIL4.
    \item \textbf{Medium term (12 months):} Formal convergence proofs. Federated
          OIL with DP. Codebook learning theory guarantees. Prototype SNN-OIL
          weight encoding on neuromorphic simulators.
    \item \textbf{Long term (24+ months):} Sub-1.5\,BPW training via
          lottery-ticket activation. Tapeout OIL ASIC. Deploy SNN-OIL hybrid.
          Break the 1.0\,BPW barrier through temporal or ensemble encoding.
\end{enumerate}
This programme builds on Chapters~2--5 and Chapter~9, charting a path
from proof-of-concept to a mature quantised training ecosystem spanning
digital, neuromorphic, and federated paradigms.
